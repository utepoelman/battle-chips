Maths Background & Placement Strategy

Two-dice sample space, position weights & counter deployment algorithm

1. The Two-Dice Sample Space & Position Weights

When rolling two fair six-sided dice, there are 6 × 6 = 36 equally likely ordered outcomes (d1, d2), so each ordered pair has a theoretical probability of 1/36.

Each ruleset maps those 36 outcomes onto board columns. Every column s is given a whole-number position weight w(s) = 36 × P(roll s), which equals the exact number of two-dice combinations out of 36 that land on that column:

  • Standard Sum: Forms a symmetric triangular distribution where values near the centre can be rolled in more ways than extreme outer numbers.
  • Absolute Difference: Maps identical rolls onto zero and non-zero gaps onto positive integers, creating an uneven weight distribution across the columns.
  • Maximum & Minimum: Produce linearly skewed distributions where the position weight increases steadily from one end of the board to the other.
  • Odd/Even Sum & Product: Use just two columns (Even and Odd). For the sum both have weight 18; for the product Even has weight 27 and Odd only 9, since both dice must be odd.

2. Strike Coverage vs. Survival Depth & Weighted Points

Analysing the trade-off between triggering strikes and surviving to score points is the core mathematical tension of Battle Chips:

Single-Roll Strike Coverage

P(Strike) = Σ (w(s) / 36)  for cs ≥ 1

Because one roll triggers at most one strike, placing your first counter on an empty column unlocks w(s)/36 single-roll strike probability. Adding extra counters to that same column adds zero extra immediate strike chance—instead, it buys survival insurance against rival attacks.

Position-Weighted Point Score

Points = Σ cs · w(s)

If a match ends at the Round Limit or via End Early, each surviving counter on space s earns w(s) points. Here, every surviving counter contributes its full column weight directly to your total score.

3. Algorithm for Placing Counters at the Start

Rather than guessing where to deploy your N counters, use this 5-step sequential decision algorithm to choose each counter's position systematically:

  1. Find the Position Weights: List all 36 ordered dice pairs (d1, d2), apply the chosen ruleset formula, and count how many pairs land on each column s to determine its whole-number weight w(s) = 36 × P(s). Start with cs = 0 counters on every space.
  2. Place Counters One at a Time: Assign your N counters sequentially (k = 1, 2, …, N), re-evaluating the board after each counter is placed.
  3. Evaluate Marginal Coverage vs. Survival Depth: For every candidate column s, estimate the marginal gain of adding one more counter:
    • Empty Column (cs = 0): Placing a counter unlocks a brand-new strike trigger worth w(s)/36 in immediate strike probability, plus w(s) potential end-game points if it survives.
    • Occupied Column (cs ≥ 1): Placing an additional counter adds 0 immediate single-roll strike probability, so value it by its diminishing-returns survival depth—protecting a high-weight trigger from being silenced and preserving w(s) points for a Round Limit or Early End.
  4. Compare Marginal Gain Across All Spaces: Factor in opponent targeting behaviour (observant rivals target high-weight columns first to shut down your best triggers), choose the single column s* with the greatest net marginal gain, and increment cs* by 1.
  5. Repeat & Adjust for Game Length: Repeat Steps 3–4 until all N counters are placed. When playing with a short Round Limit or against multiple opponents, place greater emphasis on survival depth on high-weight columns; in longer elimination matches, favour broader strike coverage across distinct columns.

Battle Chips: The Last Chip Standing - Rules

Free-Target Elimination Mechanic

All players deploy counters across board spaces. On your turn, roll the 2 dice:

  • IF you have a counter in the rolled space: You unlock a Strike! You get to select ANY opponent counter on the entire board to destroy! It does not have to be in the rolled space.
  • IF you do NOT have a counter in the rolled space: You miss and cannot attack on that turn.
  • Elimination & Spectating: When a player loses their last counter, they stop rolling and spectate the rest of the match.

Winning Condition, Round Limit & Ending Early

Eliminate all opposing counters until you are The Last Chip Standing! Every board position has a whole-number weight equal to 36 × probability of rolling that total. If the Round Limit is reached or you click End Early (completing the current round so every player has had the exact same number of rolls), each surviving counter scores its column's weight in points, and the player with the highest point total wins.

Ruleset Variants

  • Sum (2..12): Roll = Die 1 + Die 2
  • Difference (0..5): Roll = |Die 1 - Die 2|
  • Maximum (1..6): Roll = Max(Die 1, Die 2)
  • Minimum (1..6): Roll = Min(Die 1, Die 2)
  • Odd/Even (+): Roll = whether Die 1 + Die 2 is Odd or Even
  • Odd/Even (×): Roll = whether Die 1 × Die 2 is Odd or Even
